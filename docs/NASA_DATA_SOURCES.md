# NASA Data Sources and Scientific Context

This document records the official NASA sources that inform THEIA's scientific and mission-health knowledge layer. The registry is designed for local-first research review and operational context, not for medical diagnosis.

## Primary official sources

- NASA Human Research Program: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/
- Five Hazards of Human Spaceflight: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/
- Risks of Human Spaceflight: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/
- Ongoing HRP Research: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/ongoing-hrp-research/
- NASA Open Science Data Repository: https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/
- OSDR documentation: https://science.nasa.gov/biological-physical/data/osdr/
- NASA Human Research Program Evidence Reports: https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Evidence/
- NASA Human Research Roadmap: https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Risks/

## Relevance to THEIA

THEIA prioritizes datasets and research themes related to:

- Cardiovascular physiology
- Musculoskeletal health and bone loss
- Sleep and circadian rhythms
- Behavioral health and crew performance
- Immune system and stress response
- Sensorimotor and vestibular function
- Space radiation exposure and biological response
- Nutrition and metabolic adaptation
- Physiological changes in microgravity

## Scientific governance

- NASA program data are treated as research metadata and evidence context.
- Not all studies contain direct clinical measurements.
- Human, animal, and cellular datasets are not mixed without explicit labeling.
- Statistical anomalies do not constitute medical diagnoses.
- All imported data remain local and under project-controlled storage.

## Licensing and use

The project keeps NASA references, links, and metadata in a local catalog. When a dataset is imported from a file, the user is responsible for confirming local legal access and source conditions before using it operationally.
